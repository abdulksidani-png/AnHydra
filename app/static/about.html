Why we built this

An animal smaller than a grain of sand can lose almost all its water and come back to life. We wanted to know which proteins make that possible.

Tardigrades survive drying out by turning the inside of their cells into a kind of glass, holding everything in place until water returns. A few protein families do that work: CAHS proteins in tardigrades, and LEA proteins and dehydrins in plants. If we could find more proteins like them, we could help vaccines, medicines and crops survive without refrigeration. Keeping medicine cold is one of the hardest parts of getting it to the people who need it.

The search is slow. Labs can only test a handful of candidates, and there are millions of known protein sequences. We came to SwampHacks XII wanting to build something with real science underneath it, not just a demo. A first-pass filter seemed like a problem a team of four could take seriously in a weekend: paste a sequence, find out whether it looks like one of the protective families, and see why.

Along the way, the project taught us something we didn't expect. Our model scored 0.987 on the test we had designed to be hard. Instead of celebrating, we went looking for the catch, and found it: our "negative" examples were mostly folded proteins, so the task was easier than it looked. We rebuilt the data and reported the honest number. That became the heart of the project.

Our mission

Find the proteins that let life survive without water, and be honest about what a model can and cannot tell you. The AnHydra team
01

Triage, not prophecy

AnHydra asks “does this look like a protective family?”, never “how well will it protect?”

02

No made-up numbers

If the model can’t score, the site says so. It never shows a guess dressed up as a result.

03

Doubt good results

Our best finding came from questioning our best score, not from accepting it.

04

Explain every score

Each result shows the sequence features behind it, in plain language.
